## Assertion & Reason Type Questions (1 Mark)
### 1. Glucose Pentaacetate Reactivity
 * **Assertion (A):** The pentaacetate of glucose does not react with \text{H}_2\text{N-OH}.
 * **Reason (R):** It indicates the absence of free -\text{CHO} group in glucose.
 * **Answer:** **Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).**
> *Note:* If Reason is stated as *"presence of free -\text{CHO} group"*, then **(A) is true but (R) is false**.
> 
### 2. Complementary DNA Strands
 * **Assertion (A):** The two strands of DNA are complementary to each other.
 * **Reason (R):** The hydrogen bonds are formed between specific pairs of bases.
 * **Answer:** **Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).**
   *(Adenine pairs with Thymine via 2\text{ H-bonds}, Guanine pairs with Cytosine via 3\text{ H-bonds}.)*
### 3. Glucose Oxidation with Bromine Water
 * **Assertion (A):** Glucose gets oxidized to six carbon gluconic acid on reaction with bromine water.
 * **Reason (R):** The carbonyl group is absent in the open chain structure of glucose.
 * **Answer:** **(A) is true, but (R) is false.**
   *(The oxidation specifically proves the presence of an aldehyde carbonyl group in open-chain glucose).*
### 4. Chirality and Optical Rotation of D(+)-Glucose
 * **Assertion (A):** \text{D(+)-Glucose} is dextrorotatory in nature.
 * **Reason (R):** (+) represents dextrorotatory nature and \text{D} represents the configuration.
 * **Answer:** **Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).**
   *(Dextrorotatory behavior is determined experimentally, not purely by the \text{D}-configuration assignment).*
## Theoretical & Conceptual Questions
### Key Definitions
 * **Peptide Linkage:** An amide bond (-\text{CO}-\text{NH}-) formed between the carboxyl group (-\text{COOH}) of one \alpha-amino acid and the amino group (-\text{NH}_2) of another, with the loss of a water molecule.
 * **Nucleotide:** The fundamental repeating unit of nucleic acids consisting of three components: a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group.
 * **Anomers:** Diastereomers of cyclic carbohydrates that differ in configuration only at the hemiacetal/hemiketal carbon (\text{C-1} for aldoses, \text{C-2} for ketoses), known as the **anomeric carbon** (e.g., \alpha\text{-D-glucose} and \beta\text{-D-glucose}).
 * **Invert Sugar:** An equimolar mixture of \text{D-glucose} and \text{D-fructose} obtained by the hydrolysis of sucrose. It is called "invert" because dextrorotatory sucrose (+66.5^\circ) yields a levorotatory mixture (net -19.8^\circ).
 * **Glycosidic Linkage:** An oxide linkage (-\text{O}-) that joins two monosaccharide units together through condensation (loss of a \text{H}_2\text{O} molecule).
 * **Denaturation of Proteins:** The process where a protein loses its native 3D conformation (secondary, tertiary, and quaternary structures) due to physical or chemical changes (like heat, \text{pH} changes), while keeping its primary structure intact.
 * **Oligosaccharides:** Carbohydrates that yield 2\text{ to }10 monosaccharide units upon hydrolysis (e.g., sucrose, maltose).
 * **Reducing Sugar:** Any carbohydrate containing a free aldehyde or ketone group (or a free hemiacetal/hemiketal hydroxyl group) capable of reducing Fehling's solution and Tollen's reagent (e.g., all monosaccharides, maltose, lactose).
### Key Differentiations
#### 1. Peptide Linkage vs. Glycosidic Linkage
 * **Peptide Linkage:** Covalent amide bond (-\text{CO}-\text{NH}-) connecting amino acids in proteins.
 * **Glycosidic Linkage:** Ether-type oxide bond (-\text{O}-) connecting monosaccharide units in carbohydrates.
#### 2. Essential vs. Non-Essential Amino Acids
 * **Essential Amino Acids:** Cannot be synthesized by the body and must be supplied in the diet (e.g., Valine, Leucine).
 * **Non-Essential Amino Acids:** Can be synthesized in the human body; not required strictly via diet (e.g., Glycine, Alanine).
#### 3. Fibrous vs. Globular Proteins
 * **Fibrous Proteins:** Polypeptide chains run parallel; linear/thread-like structure held by strong \text{H-bonds} or disulfide bridges. Insoluble in water (e.g., Keratin, Myosin).
 * **Globular Proteins:** Polypeptide chains fold into spherical shapes. Soluble in water (e.g., Insulin, Albumin).
#### 4. Nucleotide vs. Nucleoside
 * **Nucleoside:** \text{Base} + \text{Pentose Sugar}.
 * **Nucleotide:** \text{Base} + \text{Pentose Sugar} + \text{Phosphate Group} (i.e., Nucleoside + Phosphate).
#### 5. Amylose vs. Amylopectin
 * **Amylose:** Water-soluble fraction of starch (15\text{--}20\%). Long unbranched chain of \alpha\text{-D-glucose} linked by \text{C-1}-\text{C-4} glycosidic bonds.
 * **Amylopectin:** Water-insoluble fraction of starch (80\text{--}85\%). Branched chain with \text{C-1}-\text{C-4} glycosidic main chains and \text{C-1}-\text{C-6} glycosidic branch points.

## Reactions & Structure of Glucose (2-3 Marks)
### Chemical Reactions of \text{D-Glucose}
 1. **Reaction with \text{HI} (Prolonged Heating):** Proves that all 6 carbon atoms are linked in a straight chain.
   
 2. **Reaction with Bromine Water (\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}):** Mild oxidation confirming the carbonyl group is an **aldehyde**.
   
 3. **Reaction with Concentrated \text{HNO}_3:** Oxidation of both aldehyde and primary alcohol groups to yield a dicarboxylic acid.
   
 4. **Reaction with Hydroxylamine (\text{H}_2\text{N-OH}):** Confirms presence of a carbonyl group (>\text{C=O}).
   
 5. **Reaction with Acetic Anhydride [(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}]:** Acetylation confirms presence of **five hydroxyl (-\text{OH}) groups**.
   
 6. **Reaction with Hydrogen Cyanide (\text{HCN}):** Addition reaction proving presence of a carbonyl group.
   
### Proving Structural Features of Glucose
 * **Absence of free -\text{CHO} in cyclic form:**
   Despite having an aldehyde in its open chain, Glucose **does not** give Schiff's test, does not form hydrogen sulfite addition products with \text{NaHSO}_3, and its **pentaacetate does not react with \text{H}_2\text{N-OH}**. This proves that the -\text{CHO} group participates in cyclic hemiacetal formation.
