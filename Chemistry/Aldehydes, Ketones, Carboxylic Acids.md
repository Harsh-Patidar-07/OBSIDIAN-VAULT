### Acidic Cleavage of Unsymmetrical Ethers with HI

#### Case: When one alkyl group is 3° (Tertiary)
When one of the alkyl groups is tertiary, the reaction proceeds via an **S_N1 mechanism** due to the formation of a highly stable 3° carbocation. The iodide ion attacks the tertiary group.

**Reaction:**
$$(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-CH}_3 + \text{HI} \rightarrow (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I} + \text{CH}_3\text{OH}$$

#### Summary Table for Ether Cleavage with Limited HI

| Nature of Alkyl Groups                           | Mechanism | Where does I⁻ go?                             | Where does -OH go?               |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :-------- | :-------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- |
| Both are **1° or 2°**                            | S_N2      | **Smaller / less hindered** carbon            | Larger group                     |
| One group is **3°**                              | S_N1      | **3° carbon** (forms stable carbocation)      | Smaller group                    |
| One group is **Phenyl** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5$) | S_N2      | **Alkyl group** (e.g., $\text{CH}_3\text{I}$) | Stays on ring to form **Phenol** |
