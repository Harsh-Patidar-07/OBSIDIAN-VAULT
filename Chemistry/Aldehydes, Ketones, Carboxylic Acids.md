### Acidic Cleavage of Unsymmetrical Ethers with HI

#### Case: When one alkyl group is 3° (Tertiary)
When one of the alkyl groups is tertiary, the reaction proceeds via an **S_N1 mechanism** due to the formation of a highly stable 3° carbocation. The iodide ion attacks the tertiary group.

**Reaction:**
$$(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-CH}_3 + \text{HI} \rightarrow (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I} + \text{CH}_3\text{OH}$$

# Nucleophilic Addition-Elimination of Ammonia Derivatives

Instead of rote-learning dozens of complex reactions, use the **Water Elimination Trick**.

---

## 1. The Core Pattern

All derivatives (oximes, hydrazones, semicarbazones) are formed when **ammonia or a substituted ammonia derivative** ($\text{NH}_2\text{--Z}$) reacts with the **carbonyl group** ($\text{C=O}$) in a slightly acidic medium.

This is a simple **addition-elimination reaction** where a molecule of water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) is lost:

$$\text{C=O} + \text{H}_2\text{N--Z} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{C=N--Z} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

> [!tip] The General Trick
> 1. Take the two hydrogens ($\text{H}_2$) from the $\text{--NH}_2$ group.
> 2. Take the oxygen ($\text{O}$) from the carbonyl $\text{C=O}$ group.
> 3. Strip them off as $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
> 4. Join the remaining carbon directly to nitrogen with a double bond ($\text{C=N--Z}$).

---

## 2. Reagent to Derivative Mapping Matrix

You only need to know what attached group (**Z**) corresponds to which derivative name:

| Reagent ($\text{H}_2\text{N--Z}$) | Attached Group ($\text{Z}$) | Derivative Name ($\text{C=N--Z}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hydroxylamine** | $-\text{OH}$ | **Oxime** |
| **Hydrazine** | $-\text{NH}_2$ | **Hydrazone** |
| **Phenylhydrazine** | $-\text{NH--C}_6\text{H}_5$ | **Phenylhydrazone** |
| **2,4-DNP** *(Brady's Reagent)* | $-\text{NH--C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NO}_2)_2$ | **2,4-DNP Derivative** |
| **Semicarbazide** | $-\text{NH--CO--NH}_2$ | **Semicarbazone** |
| **Primary Amine** | $-\text{R}$ | **Schiff's Base** *(Substituted Imine)* |

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Reaction Builder

### Example: Reaction of Acetone with Hydroxylamine

1. **Draw Carbonyl:** $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C=O}$
2. **Align Reagent:** $\text{H}_2\text{N--OH}$
3. **Remove Water & Join:**
   $$(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C=}\mathbf{O} + \mathbf{H}_2\text{N--OH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{C=N--OH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
4. **Product Name:** Acetone oxime

---

## 4. Frequent Board/Competitive Conceptual Questions

> [!question] Q1: Why is the pH controlled (mildly acidic, pH ≈ 3.5) during this reaction?
> * **If too basic:** There are not enough $\text{H}^+$ ions to protonate the carbonyl oxygen to make it electrophilic enough for attack.
> * **If too acidic:** The ammonia derivative gets protonated to form an ammonium salt ($\text{R--NH}_3^+$), losing its lone pair and becoming non-nucleophilic.

> [!question] Q2: Semicarbazide ($\text{H}_2\text{N--NH--CO--NH}_2$) has two $-\text{NH}_2$ groups. Which one acts as the nucleophile and why?
> **Answer:**
> * The $-\text{NH}_2$ group attached directly to the carbonyl carbon ($-\text{CO--}$) is involved in **resonance** with the carbonyl group:
>   $$\text{H}_2\text{N--C}(=\text{O}) \longleftrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{N}^+=\text{C}(\text{--O}^-)$$
>   Its lone pair is delocalized and unavailable for nucleophilic attack.
> * The other $-\text{NH}_2$ group (attached to $-\text{NH--}$) is **not in resonance**, so its lone pair is localized and available to attack the aldehyde/ketone.
