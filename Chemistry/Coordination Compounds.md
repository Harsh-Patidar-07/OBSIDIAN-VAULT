### Secondary Valency 

According to Werner's coordination theory:

- **Primary valency** corresponds to the oxidation state of the central metal ion.
    
- **Secondary valency** corresponds to the **coordination number** of the central metal ion (the total number of sigma bonds formed between the ligands and the metal ion).
    

In the complex ion $[Co(NH_3)_5(NO_2)]^{2+}$:

- $NH_3$ (ammine) is a **monodentate** ligand, and there are 5 of them.
    
- $NO_2^-$ (nitrito-N) is also a **monodentate** ligand, and there is 1 of them.
    

$$\text{Secondary Valency} = \text{Total number of monodentate ligands} = 5 + 1 = 6$$

Therefore, the secondary valency of Cobalt ($Co$) in this complex is **6**.

### The Spectrochemical Series Order

$$I^- < Br^- < S^{2-} < SCN^- \text{ (S-bonded)} < Cl^- < N_3^- < F^- < OH^- < C_2O_4^{2-} \text{ (ox)} \approx H_2O < NCS^- \text{ (N-bonded)} < EDTA^{4-} < NH_3 < en < bpy < phen < NO_2^- < CH_3^- < C_6H_5^- < CN^- < CO$$

- **Weak Field Ligands (WFL):** Halogen and Oxygen donors ($\text{F}^-$, $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{OH}^-$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{ox}^{2-}$). Do not force electron pairing.
    
- **Strong Field Ligands (SFL):** Nitrogen and Carbon donors ($\text{NH}_3$, $\text{en}$, $\text{NO}_2^-$, $\text{CN}^-$, $\text{CO}$). Force electron pairing.

### Critical Rules and Exceptions

#### 1. $4d$ and $5d$ Transition Metals

- **Rule:** For any central metal ion belonging to the $4d$ or $5d$ series (e.g., $\text{Pt}$, $\text{Pd}$, $\text{Rh}$, $\text{Ir}$), the crystal field splitting energy ($\Delta_o$) is exceptionally large.
    
- **Consequence:** **All ligands act as Strong Field Ligands (SFL)** and force electron pairing, regardless of their position in the spectrochemical series.
    

#### 2. $\text{Co}^{3+}$ Exceptions

- $\text{Co}^{3+}$ has a high positive charge density, which enhances splitting.
    
- **Oxalate ($\text{ox}^{2-}$)** acts as an SFL when complexed with $\text{Co}^{3+}$, forcing electron pairing to create a low-spin, diamagnetic configuration ($t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$).
    
- Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) sits on the borderline but generally acts as a WFL unless specified. $\text{NH}_3$ acts as an SFL with $\text{Co}^{3+}$.

## Determining Magnetic Properties

1. **Calculate the Oxidation State** of the central metal ion.
    
2. **Determine the d-electron configuration** (e.g., Fe2+=3d6, Cu2+=3d9).
    
3. **Identify the Metal Period:** If the metal is 4d or 5d, force pairing automatically.
    
4. **Evaluate Ligand Strength via Donor Atom:**
    
    - C or N donor → SFL (Force pairing).
        
    - O or Halogen donor → WFL (No pairing unless rule 1 or 2 applies).
        
5. **Fill the Orbitals:**
    
    - **SFL (Low Spin):** Pair electrons completely in the lower energy orbitals (t2g​) before placing any in the higher energy orbitals (eg​).
        
    - **WFL (High Spin):** Fill all five orbitals singly before pairing any electrons.
        
6. **Evaluate Magnetism:**
    
    - Unpaired electrons ≥1→ **Paramagnetic**
        
    - Unpaired electrons =0→ **Diamagnetic**
