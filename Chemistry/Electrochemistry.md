# Ultimate Guide to Electrolysis Products

##  The Core Setup
* **Cathode (-):** Attracts **Cations** (+). **Reduction** occurs here (Gain of electrons).
* **Anode (+):** Attracts **Anions** (-). **Oxidation** occurs here (Loss of electrons).

>  THE #1 JEE/BITSAT TRAP: Check the Electrode First!
> * **Inert Electrodes (Pt, Graphite):** Do not react. Follow the competition rules below.
> * **Active Electrodes (Cu, Ag, Ni):** The **Anode metal itself dissolves** ($M \rightarrow M^{n+} + ne^-$) because oxidizing the metal electrode is easier than oxidizing water or anions!

---

## Front 1: The Cathode Battle (Reduction)
*Rule: The species with the **higher (more positive) $E^\circ_{\text{red}}$** wins.*

### 1. Active Metals (Group 1, 2, and $Al^{3+}$)
* **Examples:** $Na^+, K^+, Mg^{2+}, Ca^{2+}, Al^{3+}$
* **Why:** Their $E^\circ_{\text{red}}$ is highly negative (e.g., $Na^+ = -2.71\text{ V}$). Water ($E^\circ_{\text{red}} = -0.83\text{ V}$) easily defeats them.
* **Winner:** Water ($H_2O$)
* **Reaction:** $2H_2O + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2(g) + 2OH^-$
* **Product:** **$H_2$ gas** (pH near cathode rises)

### 2. Less Active Metals (Below/Near Hydrogen)
* **Examples:** $Cu^{2+}, Ag^+, Ni^{2+}, Hg^{2+}$
* **Why:** Their $E^\circ_{\text{red}}$ is higher than water (e.g., $Cu^{2+} = +0.34\text{ V}$).
* **Winner:** The Metal Cation
* **Reaction:** $M^{n+} + ne^- \rightarrow M(s)$
* **Product:** **Metal plating/deposition**

### 3. Acidic Solutions
* **Winner:** $H^+$ ions
* **Reaction:** $2H^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2(g)$
* **Product:** **$H_2$ gas**

---

## Front 2: The Anode Battle (Oxidation)
*Rule: The species with the **lower (less positive) $E^\circ_{\text{red}}$** (or highest oxidation potential) wins.*

###  The Absolute Preference Order for Anodes:
$$\text{Halides } (I^- > Br^- > Cl^-) > \text{Water } (H_2O) > \text{Oxygenated Ions } (SO_4^{2-}, NO_3^-, CO_3^{2-}, F^-)$$

### 1. Halides Win Over Water
* **Examples:** Concentrated/Moderate $Cl^-, Br^-, I^-$
* **Winner:** The Halide ($X^-$)
* **Reaction:** $2X^- \rightarrow X_2 + 2e^-$
* **Product:** **Halogen gas/liquid ($Cl_2, Br_2, I_2$)**
* *Note (JEE/BITSAT):* Kinetically, water should win over $Cl^-$, but due to **overpotential** (the high kinetic energy barrier for water to release $O_2$ gas), $Cl^-$ gets discharged instead.

### 2. Water Wins Over Oxygenated Ions & Fluoride
* **Examples:** $SO_4^{2-}, NO_3^-, CO_3^{2-}, F^-$
* **Why:** These ions are heavily oxidized already and extremely stable ($E^\circ_{\text{red}}$ for $SO_4^{2-} \rightarrow S_2O_8^{2-}$ is $+2.01\text{ V}$). Water ($E^\circ_{\text{red}} = +1.23\text{ V}$) wins easily.
* **Winner:** Water ($H_2O$)
* **Reaction:** $2H_2O \rightarrow O_2(g) + 4H^+ + 4e^-$
* **Product:** **$O_2$ gas** (pH near anode drops)

## Master Summary Table (Inert Pt Electrodes)

| Electrolyte | Cathode Product | Anode Product | Net Effect on Electrolyte Solution pH |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Molten $NaCl$** | $Na(s)$ | $Cl_2(g)$ | No change (No water present) |
| **Aqueous $NaCl$ (Brine)** | **$H_2(g)$** | **$Cl_2(g)$** | **Increases** (leaves behind $Na^+$ and $OH^-$) |
| **Highly Dilute aq. $NaCl$** | **$H_2(g)$** | **$O_2(g)$** | No change (Net reaction is just splitting water) |
| **Aqueous $CuSO_4$** | **$Cu(s)$** | **$O_2(g)$** | **Decreases** (leaves behind $H^+$ and $SO_4^{2-}$ $\rightarrow$ $H_2SO_4$) |
| **Dilute $H_2SO_4$** | **$H_2(g)$** | **$O_2(g)$** | No change (Acid concentration increases slightly as water leaves) |

##  5-Second Exam Checklist
1. **Electrode type?** If Active (like Cu electrode in $CuSO_4$), stop everything $\rightarrow$ Anode dissolves, Cathode gets metal plating.
2. **State?** If molten $\rightarrow$ no water competition. Direct discharge.
3. **Cathode?** Is it a Group 1, 2, or Al metal? Yes $\rightarrow$ **$H_2$**. No $\rightarrow$ **Metal**.
4. **Anode?** Is it a heavy oxygenated ion ($SO_4^{2-}, NO_3^-$)? Yes $\rightarrow$ **$O_2$**. Is it a halide ($Cl^-, Br^-, I^-$)? Yes $\rightarrow$ **Halogen gas**.
