## Assertion & Reason Type Questions (1 Mark)

### Question 1
* **Assertion (A):** The pentaacetate of glucose does not react with $\text{H}_2\text{N-OH}$.
* **Reason (R):** It indicates the absence of free $-\text{CHO}$ group in glucose.
* **Answer:** **Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).**
> [!note]
> If a question paper variant gives Reason as *"presence of free $-\text{CHO}$ group"*, then **(A) is true but (R) is false**.

---

### Question 2
* **Assertion (A):** The two strands of DNA are complementary to each other.
* **Reason (R):** The hydrogen bonds are formed between specific pairs of bases.
* **Answer:** **Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).**
> Adenine pairs with Thymine via $2\text{ H-bonds}$ ($\text{A}=\text{T}$), and Guanine pairs with Cytosine via $3\text{ H-bonds}$ ($\text{G}\equiv\text{C}$).

---

### Question 3
* **Assertion (A):** Glucose gets oxidized to six carbon gluconic acid on reaction with bromine water.
* **Reason (R):** The carbonyl group is absent in the open chain structure of glucose.
* **Answer:** **(A) is true, but (R) is false.**
> The oxidation specifically proves the presence of an aldehyde carbonyl group in open-chain glucose.

---

### Question 4
* **Assertion (A):** $\text{D(+)-Glucose}$ is dextrorotatory in nature.
* **Reason (R):** $(+)$ represents dextrorotatory nature and $\text{D}$ represents the configuration.
* **Answer:** **Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).**
> Dextrorotatory behavior is determined experimentally by polarimetry, not derived from the spatial $\text{D}$-configuration assignment.

## Theoretical & Conceptual Questions (1–3 Marks)

### Definitions

> [!abstract] Key Definitions

1. **Peptide Linkage:** An amide bond ($-\text{CO}-\text{NH}-$) formed between the carboxyl group ($-\text{COOH}$) of one $\alpha$-amino acid and the amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) of another, with the elimination of a water molecule.
2. **Nucleotide:** The fundamental repeating unit of nucleic acids consisting of three components: a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group.
3. **Anomers:** Pair of stereoisomers of cyclic monosaccharides that differ in configuration only at the hemiacetal or hemiketal carbon ($\text{C-1}$ for aldoses, $\text{C-2}$ for ketoses), known as the **anomeric carbon** (e.g., $\alpha\text{-D-glucose}$ and $\beta\text{-D-glucose}$).
4. **Invert Sugar:** An equimolar mixture of $\text{D-glucose}$ and $\text{D-fructose}$ obtained by the acid/enzymatic hydrolysis of sucrose. It is called "invert" because dextrorotatory sucrose ($[\alpha]_\text{D} = +66.5^\circ$) changes optical rotation to a net levorotatory mixture ($[\alpha]_\text{D} = -19.8^\circ$).
5. **Glycosidic Linkage:** An oxide linkage ($-\text{O}-$) formed between two monosaccharide units via condensation with loss of a water molecule.
6. **Denaturation of Proteins:** The process where a native protein loses its higher-order spatial structure (secondary, tertiary, and quaternary) due to physical agents (heat) or chemical agents ($\text{pH}$ changes), keeping its primary structure intact.
7. **Oligosaccharides:** Carbohydrates that yield $2\text{ to }10$ monosaccharide units upon hydrolysis (e.g., sucrose, maltose, lactose).
8. **Reducing Sugar:** Any carbohydrate possessing a free aldehyde or ketone functional group (or a free hemiacetal/hemiketal hydroxyl) that can reduce Tollen's reagent or Fehling's solution (e.g., all monosaccharides, maltose, lactose).

---

### Key Differentiations

#### 1. Peptide Linkage vs. Glycosidic Linkage
| Feature | Peptide Linkage | Glycosidic Linkage |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature of Bond** | Amide bond ($-\text{CO}-\text{NH}-$) | Oxide / Ether bond ($-\text{O}-$) |
| **Occurs In** | Proteins / Polypeptides | Carbohydrates (Di/Polysaccharides) |
| **Monomer Units** | Linked $\alpha$-amino acids | Linked monosaccharide units |

#### 2. Essential vs. Non-Essential Amino Acids
| Category | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Essential Amino Acids** | Cannot be synthesized by the body; must be obtained through diet. | Valine, Leucine, Lysine |
| **Non-Essential Amino Acids** | Can be synthesized in the body from metabolic intermediates. | Glycine, Alanine, Aspartic acid |

#### 3. Fibrous vs. Globular Proteins
| Property | Fibrous Protein | Globular Protein |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Polypeptide chains run parallel to form linear thread-like structures. | Polypeptide chains fold around to give compact spherical shapes. |
| **Solubility** | Insoluble in water. | Soluble in water. |
| **Examples** | Keratin, Myosin, Collagen | Insulin, Albumin, Haemoglobin |

#### 4. Nucleotide vs. Nucleoside
| Component | Nucleoside | Nucleotide |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition** | $\text{Base} + \text{Pentose Sugar}$ | $\text{Base} + \text{Pentose Sugar} + \text{Phosphate Group}$ |
| **Phosphate** | Absent | Present |

#### 5. Amylose vs. Amylopectin
| Property | Amylose | Amylopectin |
|---|---|---|
| **Solubility** | Water-soluble fraction of starch ($15\text{--}20\%$). | Water-insoluble fraction of starch ($80\text{--}85\%$). |
| **Structure** | Long, unbranched chain of $\alpha\text{-D-glucose}$. | Highly branched chain polymer. |
| **Linkages** | $\text{C-1}-\text{C-4}$ glycosidic linkages only. | $\text{C-1}-\text{C-4}$ linear linkages + $\text{C-1}-\text{C-6}$ branch linkages. |

### Hydrolysis & Short Conceptual Answers

1. **Hydrolysis of Sucrose:**
   $$\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \underset{\text{D(+)-Glucose}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6} + \underset{\text{D(-)-Fructose}}{\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6}$$

2. **Hydrolysis of Lactose:**
   $$\text{Lactose} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Lactase}} \beta\text{-D-Galactose} + \beta\text{-D-Glucose}$$

3. **Sugar in Sugarcane:**
   * **Name:** Sucrose ($\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11}$).
   * **Hydrolysis Products:** $\alpha\text{-D-Glucose}$ and $\beta\text{-D-Fructose}$.
   * **Reducing Nature:** Non-reducing sugar because both functional groups participate in glycosidic bonding.

4. **Hydrolysis of DNA Thymine Nucleotide:**
   Yields three specific products:
   - Nitrogenous base: **Thymine**
   - Sugar: **2-deoxy-D-ribose**
   - **Phosphoric acid** ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$)

5. **Structural Differences (DNA vs. RNA):**
   - **DNA:** Sugar is $2\text{-deoxy-D-ribose}$; Bases present are $\text{A, G, C, T}$.
   - **RNA:** Sugar is $\text{D-ribose}$; Bases present are $\text{A, G, C, U}$.

6. **Why Glycogen is called "Animal Starch":**
   Its structural architecture is highly branched, similar to amylopectin, but with significantly shorter and more frequent branch points. It acts as the primary reserve carbohydrate in animal liver and muscle tissues.

7. **Effect of Denaturation on Protein Structure:**
   Disrupts hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions causing unfolding of $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ structures. Primary covalent sequence remains intact. Common $2^\circ$ structures are **$\alpha$-helix** and **$\beta$-pleated sheet**.

8. **Basic Building Units:**
   * **Proteins:** $\alpha\text{-amino acids}$
   * **Nucleic Acids:** $\text{Nucleotides}$

9. **Amphoteric Behavior of Amino Acids:**
   In aqueous solution, the acidic carboxyl group ($-\text{COOH}$) loses a proton while the basic amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) gains a proton to form a dipolar internal salt known as a **Zwitterion**:
   $$\text{H}_2\text{N}-\text{CH(R)}-\text{COOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_3\text{N}^+-\text{CH(R)}-\text{COO}^-$$
   Because it contains both acidic ($-\text{NH}_3^+$) and basic ($-\text{COO}^-$) centers, it exhibits amphoteric behavior.

10. **Vitamin Examples & Deficiency:**
    * **Fat-soluble vitamins:** Vitamin A, Vitamin D, Vitamin E, Vitamin K.
    * **Water-soluble vitamins:** Vitamin B-complex, Vitamin C.
    * **Deficiency of Vitamin A:** Night blindness (Nyctalopia) and Xerophthalmia.

## Reactions & Structure of Glucose (2–3 Marks)

### Chemical Reactions of $\text{D-Glucose}$

1. **Reaction with Prolonged Heating of $\text{HI}$:**  
   Proves that all $6$ carbon atoms are linked in a straight chain.
   $$\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CHO} \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{HI}} \underset{\text{n-Hexane}}{\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3}$$

2. **Reaction with Bromine Water ($\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$):**  
   Mild oxidation confirming the carbonyl group is an **aldehyde**.
   $$\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CHO} \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}} \underset{\text{Gluconic acid}}{\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{COOH}}$$

3. **Reaction with Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$:**  
   Oxidizes both the aldehyde and the primary hydroxyl group to give a dicarboxylic acid.
   $$\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CHO} \xrightarrow{\text{Conc. }\text{HNO}_3} \underset{\text{Saccharic acid}}{\text{HOOC}-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{COOH}}$$

4. **Reaction with Hydroxylamine ($\text{H}_2\text{N-OH}$):**  
   Confirms the presence of a carbonyl group ($>\text{C=O}$).
   $$\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CHO} + \text{H}_2\text{N-OH} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Glucose oxime}}{\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CH=N-OH}} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$

5. **Reaction with Acetic Anhydride [$(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O}$]:**  
   Acetylation confirms the presence of **five hydroxyl ($-\text{OH}$) groups**.
   $$\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CHO} + 5\,(\text{CH}_3\text{CO})_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{ZnCl}_2} \underset{\text{Glucose pentaacetate}}{\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_2-(\text{CHOCOCH}_3)_4-\text{CHO}} + 5\,\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$$

6. **Reaction with Hydrogen Cyanide ($\text{HCN}$):**  
   Addition reaction proving the presence of a carbonyl group.
   $$\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CHO} + \text{HCN} \longrightarrow \underset{\text{Glucose cyanohydrin}}{\text{HOCH}_2-(\text{CHOH})_4-\text{CH(OH)CN}}$$

### Structural Proof Questions

> [!question] How to prove the absence of a free $-\text{CHO}$ group in cyclic glucose?
> Despite possessing an aldehyde group in open form, glucose **does not** give Schiff's test, **does not** form hydrogen sulfite addition products with $\text{NaHSO}_3$, and its **pentaacetate derivative does not react with $\text{H}_2\text{N-OH}$**. This confirms that the $-\text{CHO}$ group is bound in a hemiacetal ring structure.
