For an equilateral triangle of side length $a$ inscribed in a circle of radius $R$:

$$R = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}} \implies a = R\sqrt{3}$$

**Image of a point $(x_1, y_1)$ in a line $ax + by + c = 0$:**

$$\frac{x' - x_1}{a} = \frac{y' - y_1}{b} = -2\frac{a x_1 + b y_1 + c}{a^2 + b^2}$$

**Circle Theorem:** The angle subtended by a chord at the centre is twice the angle subtended at the circumference

The perpendicular from the centre to the chord bisects this central angle


---

The point of intersection $P$ of the **internal common tangents** divides the line segment joining the centres $C_1 C_2$ **internally** in the ratio of their radii:

$$\frac{C_1 P}{P C_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2}$$
