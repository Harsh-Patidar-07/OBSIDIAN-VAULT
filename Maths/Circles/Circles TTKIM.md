The point of intersection $P$ of the **internal common tangents** divides the line segment joining the centres $C_1 C_2$ **internally** in the ratio of their radii:

$$\frac{C_1 P}{P C_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2}$$


---

- The two types of common tangents are:
    
    - Direct Common Tangent: $L_{\text{DCT}} = \sqrt{d^2 - (r_1 - r_2)^2}$
        
    - Transverse (Internal) Common Tangent: $L_{\text{TCT}} = \sqrt{d^2 - (r_1 + r_2)^2}$
        
- Since $(r_1 + r_2)^2 > (r_1 - r_2)^2$, the **transverse common tangent is always the shorter segment**.

Two circles touch each other if the distance between their centres $d$ satisfies:

$$d = \vert{}r_1 \pm r_2\vert{}$$

($+$ for external contact, $-$ for internal contact).

To find the number of common tangents between two circles, compare the distance between their centres ($d$) with the sum and difference of their radii ($r_1 + r_2$ and $\vert{}r_1 - r_2\vert{}$):

- If $d > r_1 + r_2$: Circles are separate $\implies$ **4 common tangents** (2 direct, 2 transverse).
    
- If $d = r_1 + r_2$: Circles touch **externally** $\implies$ **3 common tangents** (2 direct, 1 transverse at the point of contact).
    
- If $\vert{}r_1 - r_2\vert{} < d < r_1 + r_2$: Circles intersect at two points $\implies$ **2 common tangents** (both direct).
    
- If $d = \vert{}r_1 - r_2\vert{}$: Circles touch **internally** $\implies$ **1 common tangent**.
    
- If $d < \vert{}r_1 - r_2\vert{}$: One circle is inside the other $\implies$ **0 common tangents**.
