Given (n) different objects, the number of ways to selecting atleast one of them is : $$2^n - 1$$

---


Twin primes are pairs of prime numbers that have a difference of 2.

Example :
- Primes in $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$ are $\{2, 3, 5, 7\}$.
    
- Twin prime pairs are: $(3, 5)$ and $(5, 7)$.


---

- **Combination (${^n}C_r$): Selection only.**
    
    You are putting objects into an identical basket or forming an unranked committee. Once chosen, who stands where does not matter.
    
- **Permutation (${^n}P_r$): Selection + Assignment/Ordering.**
    
    $${^n}P_r = {^n}C_r \times r!$$
    
    You select $r$ items AND arrange them among the $r$ spots.


---

Division into groups of unequal sizes: $$\frac{n!}{a!\,b!\,c!}$$

---

Distributing $n$ identical objects among $r$ distinct recipients:

$${^{n + r - 1}}C_{r - 1}$$

### **Derangement Formula**

$$D_n = n! \left(1 - \frac{1}{1!} + \frac{1}{2!} - \frac{1}{3!} + \dots + \frac{(-1)^n}{n!}\right)$$

Standard derangement values to keep handy:

- $D_1 = 0$
    
- $D_2 = 1$
    
- $D_3 = 2$
    
- $D_4 = 9$
    
- $D_5 = 44$
