# Friction Retardation Formula: $a = \mu_k g$

## Formula
$$a = \mu_k g$$

Where:
- $a$ = Magnitude of deceleration/acceleration due to kinetic friction
- $\mu_k$ = Coefficient of kinetic friction
- $g$ = Acceleration due to gravity


## When It Is Applicable
This formula holds **only** when all of the following conditions are simultaneously met:
- **Flat Horizontal Surface:** The motion occurs entirely on a horizontal plane ($\theta = 0^\circ$).
- **Weight Equals Normal Reaction:** No other vertical forces act on the body, so $N = mg$.
- **No Other Horizontal Forces:** Friction is the sole unbalanced horizontal force acting on the body (e.g., a block sliding freely until it stops).
- **Inertial Reference Frame:** The surface is stationary or moving at a constant velocity (no vertical pseudo-forces).

## Common Exceptions
- **Inclined Plane ($\theta$):** $a = g(\sin\theta \pm \mu_k\cos\theta)$
- **Vertical/Angled External Force ($F$ at angle $\theta$):** $N \neq mg \implies a \neq \mu_k g$
- **Accelerating Elevators ($a_0$):** $a = \mu_k(g \pm a_0)$
