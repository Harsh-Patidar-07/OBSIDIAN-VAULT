# Physics: Tension Across a Pulley

### Core Principle
A difference in tension $(T_2 - T_1)$ is strictly required to provide the **net torque** that rotates a real pulley.

$$\tau_{\text{net}} = (T_2 - T_1)r = I\alpha$$

### Case Comparison
| Pulley Type | Mass & Inertia | Friction on String | Tension Relation | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ideal** | Massless ($I = 0$) | Frictionless | $T_1 = T_2$ | No net torque needed ($\tau = 0$) |
| **Real / Massive** | Has mass ($I > 0$) | Rough (No slip) | $T_1 \neq T_2$ | Net torque needed to accelerate rotation |

### Rule of Thumb
- **String over a massless pulley?** Single continuous tension $T$.
- **Pulley has mass / radius / $I$ given?** Treat the left side ($T_1$) and right side ($T_2$) as distinct tensions.
